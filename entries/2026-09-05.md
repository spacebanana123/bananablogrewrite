# 1989 (Taylor's Version)
review_score 9
1989 (Taylor's Version) is a re-recorded album by Taylor Swift. I haven't listened to much Taylor Swift overall. During the period that Taylor Swift would have been relevant, I was not into music. To be honest, for a long time I had though that I wasn't a music person, but the truth is more likely that I didn't want to spend time exploring new interests and found it easier to define myself as not a music person. 
In short, I listened to 1989 (Taylor's Version) as a person that never knew 1989 (Big Machine Records' Version). I did know some of the songs (it is hard to have literally never listened to Shake It Off or Bad Blood), but most of the album was new to me. 
The first thing that really surprised me is that these songs do not have any country style in my mind. Electronic synthesizers dominate much of the music's texture in my mind. It is really interesting to me that I assume at some point a shift from country to more pop happened, but I have truly no idea when. 
The second thing is that while I find the most popular singles (Shake It Off and Bad Blood) are good, my favorite song was Welcome to New York, a song that I haven't heard much of in the popular consciousness. I enjoy its use of synthesizers and it generally just has a vibe that I feel resonates with me, but I'm not super sure why. 
My final big thought is that I truly feel some of the songs in this album suffer from a kind of semantic satiation (song saturation?) where I feel the songs can kind of lose their meaning because I've heard it so many times. Shake It Off, for me, doesn't really inspire any emotional thought besides "oh thats the pretty good Taylor Swift song." I wonder if all songs, given enough time, will have this effect. To give another example of what I am trying to explain, Beethoven's Symphony No. 5 has this effect for me, particularly the "da-da-da-dum" at the beginning. To be honest, it is a little sad that I don't quite feel what I am sure is a pretty impactful message from Shake It Off, but it seems an inevitable function of the song's fame. 
Overall, I liked 1989 (Taylor's Version) a lot. None of the songs in the album feel like they are out of place and it does feel like the listening experience of the album as a whole is well thought out besides just being a collection of singles. I gave it a 9 out of 10 and added Welcome to New York to my main playlist.

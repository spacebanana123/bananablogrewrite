# Assembling Symbols Into My Own Poetry
review_score 8
Assembling Symbols Into My Own Poetry by Alice Longyu Gao is a pretty interesting set of songs. I choose this album because I wanted to experiment some more with hyperpop artists as I think my taste in music is closer to crystalizing these days. Anyways, I generally think that I enjoyed this album. I like the catchiness of the songs and there is a sense of artistry throughout. I think I like the tones and texture of hyperpop in general, which made a lot of the beats in the album feel nice to me. 
One thought I now have about hyperpop from this album is that sometimes it makes me wonder if my audio is malfunctioning. I have a decent pair of headphones and when thes album made use of electronic noise sounds that felt a lot like my headphone peaking I got concerned that something was wrong. This was particularly noticable in "Message Recieved"
The other comment that I have is there is a decent chunk of the songs that I felt aren't for me. That makes sense because this album discusses being lesbian a lot, which is also a theme with in Alice Longyu Gao's other work. Either way, I think I've more than mauled the dead horse on how I feel about divergent cultural experiences in the art I consume. In short, I appreciate the artistry, but find it very difficult to connect. 
Alice Longyu Gao is Chinese, which was interesting. I don't personally find that the locus of this album was on being Chinese. It was definitely a part and the first song "Little Piggy" makes use of Chinese, but I don't think I would consider it a major theme. Either way, I don't think any of the Chinese characteristics really superceded my thoughts above, but it was at least worth mentioning. 
Overall, I gave this album an 8 out 10. I didn't really connect with it on a deep emotional level, but I would enjoy relistening to it. My favorite was "Clingy" which I added to my playlist.
